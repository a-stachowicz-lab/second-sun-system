DIAGNOSIS

One method. Seven domains.

The Principle

System → Conflict → Physical Field → Measurable Quantity
→ Measurement Principle → Evidence → Rule Evaluation
→ Architectural Answer → System Status

The conflict determines what must be measured. A galvanic conflict may require electrochemical measurement. A mechanical conflict may be examined through stress, strain or deformation. A thermal conflict may require temperature or heat flux. The architecture of analysis remains the same. The physical measurement follows the actual system and the actual conflict.

Evidence

All analyses are based on publicly available scientific and technical sources. The sources provide external evidence. The system architecture structures this evidence and reconstructs conflicts, dependencies and possible architectural answers from it.

E1 — MEASUREDDirectly measured under defined conditions.
E2 — DOCUMENTEDSupported by traceable scientific or technical sources.
E3 — CONFIRMEDSupported by multiple independent evidence sources.
E4 — HYPOTHESISTechnically plausible, not yet experimentally verified.

If the available data is insufficient: UNRESOLVED — INSUFFICIENT DATA. Missing data is not estimated. Assumptions are not converted into facts.
